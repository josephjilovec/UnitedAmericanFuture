Skyway Infrastructure & Logistics Initiative

Before a drone flies, a delivery needs a reason, a safe handoff, and someone accountable at both ends. Skyway proposes grounded feasibility work for medical, emergency, and hard-to-reach logistics.

The question before the project

The aircraft is one part
of the delivery.

A fast flight does not help if packaging, dispatch, acceptance, or the return trip fails. Skyway begins by comparing the entire delivery chain with the existing ground service, then asking whether aerial transport solves a problem worth the added complexity.

Who a first study is for
A health-system logistics team, emergency-management partner, rural service organization, or qualified operator with a specific public-interest delivery problem.

Begin with the ground process

Five handoffs
that have to hold.

A proposed study model. The initial exercise would use synthetic records and closed, unregulated test cargo, with no flight.

  1. 01

    Prepare

    Define the item, packaging requirements, sending owner, and acceptance criteria.

  2. 02

    Release

    Record who can dispatch and how a missing or delayed release is handled.

  3. 03

    Transport

    Compare the complete ground baseline; separately review any proposed flight operation.

  4. 04

    Receive

    Name the receiving owner and what happens when the destination cannot accept delivery.

  5. 05

    Reconcile

    Close the custody record, investigate exceptions, and preserve the ground fallback.

The deciding question

Would this still be useful
if we never flew?

If the ground exercise reveals a better dispatch process or a simpler route, that is a valid study outcome. Flight should earn its complexity.

Public-interest logistics / closer look

A handoff needs
an accountable owner.

Packaging, custody, temperature requirements, privacy, and acceptance belong to the full delivery system. A photograph of an insulated case does not establish a validated clinical transport process.

Any later medical or biological shipment would need specialist review and the sending and receiving organizations’ approval before a delivery proposal could advance.

From concept to any future flight

Need. Ground rehearsal.
Then an authorization decision.

Qualified operators would determine the rules, permissions, aircraft, insurance, sites, and safety requirements for the specific operation. Community concerns about noise, privacy, and land use belong in that review.

No carrier, beyond-visual-line-of-sight, airspace, or clinical transport authority is established by this proposal.

A concrete first step

Route Safety & Handoff Readiness Brief

Proposed scope: one defined delivery use case between two willing receiving organizations. A commissioned study would need a named owner, qualified reviewers, agreed deliverables, real quotes, and a capped budget before work begins.

01

Delivery need and ground baseline

Define the item class, urgency, route, existing service, delivery failures, and the alternatives to flying.

02

Handoff and exception model

A documented responsibility chain for preparation, custody, acceptance, delay, failed delivery, and safe ground fallback.

03

Permission and partner brief

A proposed route-screening scope, community questions, operator requirements, cargo restrictions, and a gated demonstration concept.

This is a proposed deliverable, not an available funded engagement or an announcement of a selected location. Fees, eligibility, and delivery dates would be set only for an agreed scope.

How the proposal advances

Evidence before
the next commitment.

A study may recommend a smaller intervention, further research, or no project. Each is a useful outcome when the reasoning is clear.

  1. 01

    Prove the delivery need

    Compare complete ground-service performance and the consequences of a missed handoff.

  2. 02

    Rehearse on the ground

    Use synthetic records and closed, unregulated test cargo. Review custody, acceptance, and exceptions without flight.

  3. 03

    Review permissions and operator fit

    Qualified specialists assess the actual operation, sites, community effects, cargo, and applicable authorizations.

  4. 04

    Decide on a separately approved test

    Proceed only with a signed scope and all necessary approvals; otherwise improve the ground process or stop.

Before we begin

A few practical
questions.

Is Skyway a drone delivery operator?+

No. This is a UAF feasibility and coordination concept. A qualified operator would need to lead any future flight activity under the rules and authorizations applicable to the specific operation.

Can medical samples be used in a first demonstration?+

The proposed first exercise uses closed, unregulated test cargo and synthetic records on the ground. Any patient-related, biological, dangerous-goods, or temperature-sensitive shipment requires separate specialist, organizational, and regulatory review.

Is a remote pilot certificate enough?+

The necessary approvals depend on the actual operation. A small-drone pilot qualification alone does not establish authority for every delivery or beyond-visual-line-of-sight service. The operator must document the applicable pathway.
