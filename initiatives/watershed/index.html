Continental Watershed & River Restoration Initiative

A river carries the consequences of decisions made across an entire basin. This initiative starts with one reach: the evidence already available, the pressures that matter, and restoration options a local owner could maintain.

The question before the project

Start with a reach.
Keep the basin in view.

More measurements do not automatically produce a better restoration decision. The study begins with existing records, land and maintenance responsibilities, seasonal context, and the question a manager needs answered. New monitoring is justified only where it can change that decision.

Who a first study is for
A watershed organization, municipality, conservation district, utility, or eligible public sponsor responsible for a specific water-management decision.

Read the river as a system

Upstream decisions.

Where does the pressure begin?

A concrete first step

Watershed Baseline & Restoration Opportunity Brief

Proposed scope: one agreed river reach or small catchment with an accountable local partner. A commissioned study would need a named owner, qualified reviewers, agreed deliverables, real quotes, and a capped budget before work begins.

01

Reach evidence register

A bounded catchment, existing data with dates and quality limits, land/access constraints, and a map of unresolved questions.

02

Restoration options record

Compare source control, maintenance, riparian or erosion work, additional study, and a do-minimum alternative with qualified reviewers.

03

Monitoring and delivery brief

State what to measure, why, who approves the quality plan, who owns the land and maintenance, and what would justify follow-on work.

This is a proposed deliverable, not an available funded engagement or an announcement of a selected location. Fees, eligibility, and delivery dates would be set only for an agreed scope.

How the proposal advances

Evidence before
the next commitment.

A study may recommend a smaller intervention, further research, or no project. Each is a useful outcome when the reasoning is clear.

  1. 01

    Bound the reach and decision

    Agree on a manageable catchment, management question, and responsible local partner.

  2. 02

    Review what is already known

    Check existing public and authorized records before proposing field equipment or collection.

  3. 03

    Close a decision-critical gap

    A qualified reviewer approves methods and permissions before any new fieldwork or sampling.

  4. 04

    Choose a maintainable next step

    Compare benefit, uncertainty, land rights, cost, and maintenance; accept a brief or stop.

Before we begin

A few practical
questions.

Does this diagnose drinking-water safety or predict floods?+

No. This concept is for watershed planning and restoration readiness. It is not a drinking-water testing service, regulatory determination, or public warning system.

Will every site get sensors?+

No. Existing records and a management question come first. Equipment is considered only when a qualified reviewer can show that the information will support a useful decision and a local owner can maintain it.

How is this different from TechWater?+

This is a UAF public-interest watershed and river planning initiative. Commercial property reporting, billing analysis, and private monitoring services are separate activities and are not a condition of participation.
